VO-GO: The Vocabulary Learning App

Learn vocabulary on the go. A mobile app that empowers people to gain new words in multiple languages anywhere.

Target
Busy people, 35-45, improving career and leisure
When
A moment to oneself (5-10 minutes)
Disciplines
UX Design, Mobile app
Tools
Marvel, Loom, Skype, Google Form, Photoshop, Illustrator

Overview

VOGO lets users quickly learn definitions of words they hear and see every day, or from their own in-app categories, on their own time and anywhere: a lunch break, waiting for a meeting, the night before a presentation, or during kids' activities.

Competitive analysis

Pros:

  • Free, with no sign-up required
  • Simple navigation, clean graphics, visible learning progress
  • Accent choice (British or American), translation in the user's language, varied samples such as movies and songs, and listening practice (WordUp)
  • Build your own flashcards and spelling practice (Tinycards)

Cons:

  • Must go through every suggested word even if already known (Tinycards)
  • Limited vocabulary selection (Learn Words)
  • Occasionally cluttered UI (WordUp)
  • No pronunciation for non-English words and no language filter (Tinycards)

User research

I interviewed four people aged 35-45 with different occupations.

How they learn

  • Look up by category and use search, YouTube, LinkedIn or an online dictionary
  • Practice repetition and write words and examples down
  • Practice with friends to see real-life use

What they think

  • Hearing a word in conversation helps a lot
  • Passion makes learning fun rather than forced
  • Repetition is the most efficient way to learn
  • Beginners get more frustrated because almost every word is new

What they feel

  • Long or wordy explanations are irritating when time is short
  • Not confident without knowing how to use the word correctly
  • Words with multiple meanings are hard to place
  • Struggle when material moves too fast or examples aren't relatable

Persona: Janis Tsukasa

A 40-year-old Senior Data Analyst from LA living in Hawaii, married with one child, working 8 to 4 with lots of meetings and presentations. She learns by doing and is easily distracted.

Needs: short, frequent lessons in interesting formats (pictures, music, movies), multiple languages with pronunciation practice, dictionary-like search with categories and voice search, and the ability to write her own words and details to reuse.

Problem and hypothesis

Problem: Janis needs a way to learn new vocabulary or terms in a bite-size lesson.

Hypothesis: an app with quick search by word or category, multiple examples in video, music and TV or movie conversation, and addable, editable flashcards makes lessons convenient, easy to practice and less time-consuming.

Task analysis

Two main tasks shaped the flow: searching for language interests and categories, and building flashcards (creating them yourself or saving them from trustworthy online sources).

Crazy 8 and dot-voting

I used Crazy 8 and dot-voting to decide how the app should function based on the problem and hypothesis.

Usability testing

Improvements

  • Add 'Forgot Password' on sign-up
  • Prioritize 'Look for a Word', 'Add New', then 'Most Popular' on the home screen
  • Combine 'Play' and 'Quiz' into 'Play'
  • Show all words in a category, with subcategory filters and search
  • Add 'Add New Deck' and 'Add New Word to Existing Deck', plus search from Google or your device when adding media
  • Flag user-created cards in suggested categories
  • Add bookmarks, a pencil icon on deck covers, and Play in My Deck
  • Remove estimated learning time

View the revised prototype →

Next steps and learnings

  • More competitive analysis and interviews
  • Re-iterate the prototype and test again
  • Add the sign-up process and remaining functions, then build a higher-fidelity prototype

What I learned

  • Allocate more time to each stage, especially interviews and usability testing
  • Crazy 8 reduces design time. Once familiar, it narrows to 3-4 variations
  • Focus on function, not drawing
  • Perfection is imperfection: keep revisiting challenges, user needs and the prototype
