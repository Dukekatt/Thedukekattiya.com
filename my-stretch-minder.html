My StretchMinder

A voice-guided, immersive multimodal experience to help users maintain long-term mobility and stay healthy.

Role
Sole UX Designer and Conversation Designer
Timeline
4 months
Tools
VoiceFlow, Draw.io, Whimsical
Disciplines
VUI, Conversation Design, Feature Addition

Background

Stretching keeps muscles flexible, strong and healthy, but working adults sit around 10.5 hours a day and more than 15% of Americans have health complications from inactivity. Desk workers neglect stretching most.

Problem: despite YouTube workouts and exercise apps, daily use stays low. Sessions are long, small screens demand constant visual focus, and users can't tap the screen mid-pose without breaking it.

Solution: a hands-free multimodal experience that keeps working professionals engaged and motivated through voice guidance.

User research

I wanted to understand how spoken and written content affects exercise engagement, and how much people rely on touch commands.

Pain points

  • Finding a stretching session takes a long time (average search time over 10 minutes)
  • Sessions feel too long for a short work break
  • Pose demonstrations are visually rich, which means high cognitive load
  • Limited shortcuts create disjointed flows

Gain points

  • Short videos and GIFs show a pose quickly
  • Saving exercises, playlist-style, reduces selection time
  • Mobile access removes barriers at home or at work

Competitor and voice-app analysis

I reviewed StretchMinder, Start Stretching, and Stretch & Flexibility at Home. Strengths: bite-sized content, video plus audio guidance, freemium models, timers, personalization and reminders. Weakness: no voice commands, and skip/next only by touch.

I also reviewed four Alexa stretching skills across rating bands (6-Minute Full Body Stretch, Guided Workout: Stretch, Workout Coach, 5 Minute Posture Improvement Stretching).

  • Strengths: defined bot personalities, intent confirmation before launch, short onboarding, simple help and fallbacks
  • Weaknesses: limited conversation and few slots, inconsistent skip/next, resume doesn't return to the earlier position, little exercise variety, non-adjustable durations, weak smart-display support
Users need a short stretching guide and an easy way to interact without searching or tapping the screen, plus a way to keep the habit going through a daily routine.

The opportunity

In the existing app, users must leave their pose and touch the screen to command it. Adding voice alongside the mobile interface solves the main pain points and gives users a consistent, coach-like personality that encourages repeat use.

User: "Alexa, open Stretch Minder."
System: "Hi Pam! Nice to have you back. Do you want to stretch upper body or lower body?"
User: "Upper body, please."

Persona: Kisha Loyde

Target users are 35-60 and hold static postures for long stretches.

I work from home and run my household. I have sensitive joints and don't enjoy heavy workouts, so I want stretching as a daily routine, without fumbling with my phone, and quick.

Needs: bite-sized content, reminders, and quick access without searching.

Main features: save poses to favorites, a list of all exercises with shortcuts to poses, and quick profile access to set reminders.

Conversational repair

Some features have limited functionality, such as duration per body part. The system handles out-of-range and no-match input with hints and suggested options:

  • "Sorry, we can only spend 6 minutes on a stretch here. How long should I set the time to?"
  • "Hmmm, I don't have a specific exercise for that. Which exercise do you want to try? We can do arms, shoulders, hips, or legs."

Accessibility

A voice-first approach with a visual interface makes navigation more intuitive. Viewing upper-body stretches, saving favorites and skipping poses all work by voice, and audio instruction speeds up access to content.

Personality through voice

Stretching is calming, so the voice should be too. I used SSML to adjust tone, speed and emphasis:

<prosody rate="medium"><prosody volume="soft">
Welcome to Stretch Minder. <break strength="strong" />
I'm your stretch assistant. <break strength="strong" />
Seems like this is your first time here. <break strength="strong" />
Before we start, what is your name?
</prosody></prosody>

Prototype

I built the conversation flows in VoiceFlow, with barge-in supported at any point and appropriate responses to recognized interruptions.

Key learnings

  • Simplify scope. A narrower use case speeds up the first iterations
  • Stay on one platform. Adding diverse features shifts focus to tooling instead of scripting
  • Cover more prompts for turn-taking and no-match utterances to make the conversation more robust

The outcome helps users cut through the search process and connect to their stretching habit more easily. A multimodal interface benefits any exercise app for inclusive design, including users with visual impairments or physical handicaps.
